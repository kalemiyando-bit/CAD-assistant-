WHEEL DRIVE BENCH DEMONSTRATOR
PROOF-OF-CONCEPT DESIGN BRIEF

Revision: Archived preliminary handoff
Status: Preliminary concept only; not approved for manufacture or powered testing.
Purpose: Record the current user brief, project context, and reversible assumptions
before creating design outputs.

1. USER'S GOAL

The user is testing whether CAD Assistant can turn a conversational description
into a useful, simple engineering design draft that the user can open and edit
in Autodesk Fusion. Exact dimensions and component specifications are not
available. Choose reasonable preliminary values, keep the design simple, and
make the work in stages: prepare individual parts one at a time, then create an
assembly only after the parts have been reviewed. Do not start with a full
assembly, PCB, firmware, or manufacturing package.

2. SYSTEM CONCEPT

A small, bench-only, direct-drive wheel module:
- A wheel and hub are carried by a horizontal wheel shaft.
- A bearing and support hold the wheel shaft.
- A small brushed DC motor drives the wheel through a coupling between the
  wheel-side shaft and motor-side shaft.
- No external gear reduction is intended.
- The user describes a separate motor-side shaft; its need and dimensions
  depend on the motor ultimately selected.
- The user wants the wheel's rotational speed to be measurable.
- This is a proof of concept, not a vehicle or passenger-carrying design.

3. USER-DESCRIBED CONTROL INTENT

- A GAS button increases the speed target.
- One tap on GAS raises the target by one speed level.
- Holding GAS raises the target gradually at a steady, gentle rate.
- A BRAKE button reduces the target.
- One tap on BRAKE lowers the target by one speed level.
- Holding BRAKE gradually reduces the target to zero.
- Include a separate handbrake/emergency-stop control.
- Show or otherwise measure wheel speed.
- Do not include reverse.

Preliminary control values proposed for this proof of concept (assumptions, not
validated ratings):
- Maximum commanded wheel speed: 300 rpm.
- One tap: change the target by 25 rpm (12 levels from zero to the cap).
- Hold: ramp the target at 30 rpm per second, limited by the 300 rpm cap or zero.
- Speed feedback: measure wheel rpm and present it to the user.
- Emergency control: treat it as a latched drive-disable request pending selection
  of a motor driver and confirmation of its actual stopping behavior.

At the existing 80 mm wheel envelope, 300 rpm corresponds to a calculated rim
speed of approximately 1.26 m/s (4.52 km/h). This is a geometric calculation,
not measured performance and not a safety rating. The speed targets and control
rates must be checked against the selected motor, controller, sensor, and test
setup before implementation.

4. SOFTWARE, UNITS, AND COORDINATES

- Target CAD software: Autodesk Fusion on Windows; exact Fusion version unknown.
- The Replit environment cannot run the user's desktop Fusion application.
- The first handoff is an editable R12 DXF model-building sketch, plus dimensions
  and Fusion instructions. DXF sketch text is not a native Fusion dimension.
- Units: millimetres.
- Model frame: right-handed, Z-up; wheel/motor axis along +Y; +Z is vertical.
- The first shaft profile lies in XZ and is intended to extrude along +Y.

5. CURRENT A0 ENVELOPES AND STATUS

The project supplied these A0 envelopes:
- Wheel rim: 80 mm outside diameter x 24 mm wide, 6 mm bore.
- Hub: 30 mm outside diameter x 8 mm projection, 6 mm bore.
- Wheel-side shaft: 6 mm diameter x 46 mm long.
- Bearing envelope: 12 mm outside diameter x 6 mm inside diameter x 4 mm wide.
- Bearing support plate: 30 x 30 x 4 mm with a 12 mm center bore.
- Coupling envelope: 10 mm diameter x 10 mm long.
- Candidate motor envelope: Mabuchi FF-130SH-14230, approximately
  20.4 x 15.4 x 25.1 mm. It is not selected.

These are preliminary placeholder envelopes, not approved interface dimensions.
Fits, clearances, retention, materials, loads, component ratings, motor/coupling
interfaces, and manufacturing methods remain unresolved. No particular bearing,
wheel, coupling, motor, controller, or sensor is selected.

The supplied project brief records a 7,400 rpm no-load speed for the motor
candidate. Combined with an 80 mm wheel, that implies about 31 m/s at the rim.
This is a calculation from a candidate specification, not measured operation.
Do not use the candidate as if selected or suitable.

6. FIRST DELIVERABLE AND SEQUENCE

First deliverable:
- One-part model-building profile for the wheel-side shaft, using the existing
  A0 placeholder envelope of diameter 6 mm and length 46 mm, axis +Y.
- The profile contains only a Ø6 mm circle. The 46 mm extrusion length is stated
  separately; it is not drawn into the 2D profile.
- This choice reuses the smallest, simplest project workflow example to test the
  design handoff. It is not a claim that the shaft is mechanically adequate.
- The user can insert the DXF into an XZ sketch in Fusion, confirm millimetres
  and the Ø6 profile, then extrude 46 mm along +Y.

After the user reviews the first part, continue with the corresponding 3D shaft
handoff and then other individual parts. Candidate later parts include the wheel,
hub, motor-side shaft if required, coupling, bearing support, bearing envelope,
and motor envelope. Their order and interfaces must be resolved as the design
develops. Create the assembly only after individual parts have been reviewed.
Treat PCB, firmware, and powered testing as later work.

7. SAFETY, RESEARCH, AND LIMITATIONS

- Research scope: user-supplied sources only. Do not browse or widen the scope
  without asking.
- Provide a physical wheel guard and a current-limited test procedure before any
  powered test is recommended.
- Define and test the stop behavior with the actual motor and controller before
  powered use. A drive-disable command alone does not prove that the wheel stops
  quickly or is held.
- The 300 rpm control cap, tap step, and ramp rate are preliminary design targets,
  not a safety limit or evidence that the motor can meet them.
- The first shaft sketch is a concept handoff only. No fit, strength, balance,
  retention, thermal, electrical, safety, manufacturing, or Fusion import claim
  is made by producing the file.
- Record unavailable target-application checks as not run until the user opens
  and inspects the file in Fusion.

8. OPEN ITEMS FOR LATER STAGES

- Confirm the actual wheel, motor, bearing, coupling, controller, and sensor.
- Determine whether the motor's own shaft serves as the motor-side shaft.
- Set actual shaft interfaces, bearing support geometry, fits, retention, and
  material from chosen components and intended loads.
- Confirm the physical guard, current limit, emergency stop behavior, and safe
  powered-test procedure before considering powered operation.
- Confirm the Fusion version and inspect scale, profile, orientation, and later
  3D model dimensions in the user's Fusion installation.
